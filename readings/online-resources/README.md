## Evaluating Online Sources

### Reputable Sources Include
- Government websites (.gov)
- Universities and research institutions (.edu)
- Established organizations
- Peer-reviewed journals
- Major news outlets
- Cultural institutions (museums, archives)

### Questionable Sources to Avoid
- Wikipedia (never allowed)
- Blogs without credentials
- Unverified websites
- Biased or propaganda sites
- Sources without attribution

### Questions to Ask
1. Who authored this?
2. What are their credentials?
3. Is this peer-reviewed or edited?
4. What is the purpose of this site?
5. Is information supported by evidence?
6. When was this published/updated?
7. Can I verify this elsewhere?

## Citation Requirements

### Always Cite Online Sources
- Author (if available)
- Title of article/page
- Website name
- URL
- Access date
- Publication date

### Citation Formats
Use APA or MLA consistently:

**APA Example**:
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of article. *Website Name*. URL

**MLA Example**:
Author. "Title of Article." *Website Name*, Date, URL.

## Tips for Online Reading

### Reading Digital Content Effectively
1. Minimize distractions
2. Take notes as you read
3. Print longer articles if helpful
4. Bookmark important pages
5. Save copies of sources
6. Track citations as you go

### Managing Online Resources
- Create a folder for course readings
- Save PDFs of articles
- Keep a citation list
- Organize by week or topic
- Back up important files
