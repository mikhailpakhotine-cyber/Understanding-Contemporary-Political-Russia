# Anti-Plagiarism Guide

## What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is using someone else's words, ideas, or work without proper attribution. It is a serious academic integrity violation with severe consequences.

## Types of Plagiarism

### 1. Direct Plagiarism
Copying text word-for-word without quotation marks or citation

**Example**:
- Original: "Russia's federal system divides power between Moscow and regional governments."
- Plagiarism: Russia's federal system divides power between Moscow and regional governments.
- Correct: According to Shiraev (2013), "Russia's federal system divides power between Moscow and regional governments" (p. 45).

### 2. Paraphrasing Without Citation
Restating someone's ideas in your own words but not citing the source

**Example**:
- Original: "Political culture shapes how citizens interact with their government."
- Plagiarism: The way people engage with their government is influenced by political culture.
- Correct: Political culture influences citizen engagement with government (Shiraev, 2013).

### 3. Mosaic Plagiarism
Mixing your words with phrases from source without quotation marks

**Example**:
- Using several phrases from source throughout paragraph without indicating which are quotes

### 4. Self-Plagiarism
Reusing your own previous work without permission

### 5. Accidental Plagiarism
Forgetting to cite, incorrectly citing, or unintentionally paraphrasing

**Note**: Accidental plagiarism is still plagiarism and carries the same penalties!

## How to Avoid Plagiarism

### 1. Always Cite Your Sources
Cite when you:
- Quote directly
- Paraphrase ideas
- Use specific facts or statistics
- Reference someone's argument or theory
- Use information that isn't common knowledge

### 2. Use Quotation Marks for Direct Quotes
- Any exact wording from source must be in quotation marks
- Include citation with page number
- Keep quotes relatively short

### 3. Paraphrase Correctly
- Put ideas completely in your own words
- Don't just rearrange sentence structure
- Still cite the source
- Understand the concept before paraphrasing

### 4. Keep Track of Sources
- Note source information as you research
- Use a citation manager or organized system
- Save copies of online sources
- Record page numbers for quotes

### 5. Start Early
- Rushing leads to plagiarism mistakes
- Allow time for proper citations
- Don't wait until last minute

## Common Knowledge Exception

You don't need to cite "common knowledge" - facts widely known and easily verified.

**Common Knowledge** (no citation needed):
- Moscow is the capital of Russia
- The Soviet Union dissolved in 1991
- Russia is the largest country by land area

**Not Common Knowledge** (citation needed):
- Specific statistics about Russian economy
- Analysis of political culture
- Interpretations of historical events
- Expert opinions

## Proper Citation Examples

### Direct Quote (APA)
According to Shiraev (2013), "Political culture encompasses the attitudes and beliefs that citizens hold toward their political system" (p. 12).

### Direct Quote (MLA)
Shiraev argues that "political culture encompasses the attitudes and beliefs that citizens hold toward their political system" (12).

### Paraphrase (APA)
Russian political culture includes citizens' views on their governmental system (Shiraev, 2013).

### Paraphrase (MLA)
Russian political culture includes citizens' views on their governmental system (Shiraev 12).

## Using Turnitin

### What is Turnitin?
- Plagiarism detection software used in Canvas
- Checks your paper against billions of sources
- Generates similarity report

### How to Use It
1. Submit draft to Canvas assignment
2. Review similarity report
3. Check flagged sections
4. Revise as needed
5. Submit final version

### Understanding Similarity Reports
- Some similarity is normal (quotes, common phrases)
- High similarity (>20-30%) may indicate problems
- Review each flagged section carefully
- Proper citations should reduce similarity

### Limitations of Turnitin
- Doesn't catch all plagiarism
- May flag properly cited quotes
- Can't detect plagiarism from books not in database
- YOU are still responsible for avoiding plagiarism

## Examples: Correct vs. Incorrect

### Example 1: Direct Quote

**Incorrect** (no citation):
Russia's transition to a market economy was difficult and painful.

**Incorrect** (no quotation marks):
Shiraev says Russia's transition to a market economy was difficult and painful.

**Correct**:
According to Shiraev (2013), "Russia's transition to a market economy was difficult and painful" (p. 156).

### Example 2: Paraphrase

**Incorrect** (too similar to original, no citation):
The shift to capitalism in Russia was challenging and caused hardship.

**Incorrect** (paraphrased but no citation):
The move to a capitalist economy in Russia faced many obstacles.

**Correct**:
Russia encountered significant obstacles during its economic transition in the 1990s (Shiraev, 2013).

### Example 3: Using Multiple Sources

**Correct**:
Russia's political culture reflects both Soviet legacy and Western influences (Shiraev, 2013; Smith, 2015). While Shiraev emphasizes continuity with the past, Smith highlights the impact of globalization.
