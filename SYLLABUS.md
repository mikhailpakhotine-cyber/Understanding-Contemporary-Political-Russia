## Course Description

This course allows students to explore politics and society in contemporary Russia. The course will cover the current governance systems and policy issues. As a Foreign Culture course, INS3XXX covers Russian political culture – the attitudes and beliefs citizens hold toward their political system, country, and society. Russia's post-Soviet transition was economic as well as political, so the course will cover attitudes toward previous and current economic policies. Lastly, the course will focus on social change as it relates to democratization and capitalism. All three areas of culture – political, economic, and societal – can be compared to the United States, particularly with regard to how post-Soviet Russia's reforms and attitudes do or do not match with US systems and attitudes.

## Expectations and Rules to Remember

### Late Policy
I deduct two (2) points for each calendar day an out-of-class assignment is late. Students with serious and documented excuses will not be assessed the late penalty for the dates covered by the documentation. University-approved extracurricular activities (such as athletics) are NOT an acceptable reason for late papers; all assignments are to be completed in advance of any absence for extracurricular activities. Late assignments must be turned in before the next scheduled exam or assignment due date. Whether excused or not, late assignments submitted after the next scheduled exam or assignment is due or after the last regular class session, whichever is sooner, will not be accepted. Take-home essay exams or research papers assigned in lieu of an in-class final exam are not accepted late.

### Exam Make-Up Policy
Make-ups for in-class exams will only be given without penalty if arranged in advance or missed because of a serious and documented excuse. Make-ups due to university-approved extracurricular activities (such as athletics) will only be considered excused if documented and arranged in advance. Students unable to provide a serious, documented reason for missing an exam will be allowed to take the exam with a penalty in the form of a point deduction equivalent to at least one letter grade. It is the student's responsibility to contact me to find a mutually convenient time for the make-up. Whether excused or not, all make-up exams must be completed before the next scheduled exam or assignment due date or before the last regular class session, whichever is sooner. In-class final exams can only be made up with a serious and documented excuse.

### Late Pass
Every student has ONE "late pass." Students can be late on one assignment or take a make-up exam without a penalty, even without an excuse. This "late pass" is intended to provide flexibility and allows a one-week extension penalty-free. Students must email me by the assignment due date or exam date to notify me they are invoking their late pass. (Students who DO have a serious and documented excuse need not use their late pass.)

### Documentation of Reputable Sources
All out-of-class assignments must include proper documentation of sources. (This includes any extra credit assignments I might offer.) You may use any accepted citation style (such as APA or MLA), and you must do so correctly and consistently. For information on how to properly cite, go to Canvas (see "helpful writing resources"), visit the reference librarians in the library, or use the free tutoring services in the Writing Center.

**Grading Penalties for Citations:**
- No documentation of sources: Grade of F
- Incomplete or inconsistent documentation: Grade of D
- Failure to correctly use an accepted citation style: Grade of C

**Note**: Wikipedia and other encyclopedias are NOT allowed. Papers using Wikipedia or any obviously questionable source will receive a penalty equivalent to at least one letter grade.

### Writing and Other Out-of-Class Assignments
All out-of-class assignments must be turned in on Canvas. Always keep a backup copy on your own computer, in case of Canvas glitches or file corruption. Out-of-class assignments must be:

## Academic Integrity

### Plagiarism and Other Violations
I encourage students to study together and discuss course material with each other. However, I expect all graded work to be yours and yours alone. I require all students to adhere to the University of Central Florida's Academic Integrity Code. I will not tolerate instances of cheating, plagiarism, falsification, or other violations of the University Code.

Plagiarism has been a problem at ERAU, and I absolutely do check students' papers for it. Students caught plagiarizing will be subject to the penalties set forth by the University:
- Students will receive a grade of F for the assignment
- May receive a grade of F for the course
- Multiple violations will result in a grade of F for the course

**Note**: A grade of F for the course due to violations of academic integrity, known as a "punitive F," is permanent and cannot be removed from a student's record through grade forgiveness or any other means.

### Turnitin
Canvas includes a plagiarism-checking tool called "Turnitin." All out-of-class writing assignments have Turnitin enabled. If you submit a draft to Turnitin and find that you have plagiarized, please come see me for assistance. You will not be penalized for plagiarism in a draft. You will be penalized for plagiarism in a final assignment.

**Important**: Turnitin is a limited tool; it does not find all forms of plagiarism. You remain responsible for any form of plagiarism found in your final assignment, even if you have used Turnitin.

### Review of Draft Papers
I am willing to review hard copy drafts of out-of-class papers if they are submitted to me at least two weeks prior to the assignment's due date. I do not review drafts submitted via email.

If you come to my office during office hours to discuss a paper, I will happily answer questions, review documentation of sources, spot-check for plagiarism, and assist you in many ways. However, I will limit the review of the written draft itself to no more than 10 minutes to ensure all students visiting office hours have a chance to see me.

## Class Participation

Students are responsible for active, positive participation in all class discussions and activities. While in college, students should develop their oral communication and critical thinking skills; participation in group and class-wide discussions and activities contributes to this.

Discussion topics in political science courses can be inherently controversial. I strive to create a classroom atmosphere in which all students are comfortable making contributions and expressing their opinions. I will treat all students with respect – especially if their opinions differ from mine – and I expect the same behavior from students toward each other and toward me.

Class discussions will be analytical in nature – analytical opinions are based on reasoned, supported arguments, not emotion. I will expect you to have support for any opinion, just as you have the right to expect that of me.

### Negative Participation
Negative participation – disruptive class behavior – will result in a lowered or even failing participation grade. Disruptive behavior is broadly defined in the Student Conduct Code as "Substantial disruption of University operations including obstruction of teaching, research, administration, other University activities, and/or other authorized non-University activities which occur on campus."

Examples include but are not limited to:

- Disturbing the class when entering or leaving
